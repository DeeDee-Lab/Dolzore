# DOLZORE LIVING WORLD / HARDCORE RPG DOCTRINE
Authority: 2026-09-29 JST

## 0. Core identity

DOLZORE must support two equally valid ways to spend hundreds of hours:

### A. Adventure / combat path
A deep, demanding RPG built around:
- preparation;
- role identity;
- enemy knowledge;
- positional judgment;
- resource management;
- gear decisions;
- party cooperation;
- failure, retreat and recovery;
- long-term mastery.

### B. Life / social path
A persistent social world where players can enjoy themselves without advancing the main story or defeating enemies:
- socializing;
- fishing;
- cooking;
- gathering;
- crafting;
- trading;
- housing;
- decorating;
- BAR culture;
- collecting;
- rankings;
- exploration;
- fashion;
- community events.

Neither path is secondary.
A player who spends a month fishing, cooking and decorating a house is playing DOLZORE correctly.

Visual identity:
friendly, warm, approachable 16-bit-inspired world.

System identity:
surprisingly deep, demanding, interconnected and long-lived.

---

# 1. Design principles

## 1.1 No button-mashing
Basic attacks alone must not solve serious encounters.

Meaningful play requires:
- target selection;
- ability timing;
- positioning;
- reading enemy tells;
- status management;
- resource planning;
- party roles;
- gear preparation;
- knowing when not to fight.

## 1.2 Preparation is gameplay
Before an expedition, players may choose:
- job;
- sub-job / secondary discipline later;
- weapon;
- armor;
- food;
- tools;
- resist gear;
- consumables;
- route;
- party composition;
- time of day;
- weather conditions.

A difficult encounter may become manageable because preparation improved, not because the player gained five more levels.

## 1.3 Knowledge creates power
Enemy weaknesses, fishing conditions, recipes, trade prices and time-layer behavior are valuable knowledge.

DOLZORE rewards players who:
- record observations;
- compare notes;
- learn routes;
- notice weather;
- know NPC schedules;
- understand monster behavior.

## 1.4 Death / failure matters
Failure should create tension without destroying months of progress.

Possible penalties:
- temporary condition loss;
- repair cost;
- expedition items dropped into recoverable cache;
- weakened state;
- travel setback.

Avoid:
- permanent deletion of a normal character;
- cash-shop rescue;
- arbitrary one-shot traps with no readable clue.

## 1.5 The economy is a game system
Fishing, cooking, crafting, gathering, monster drops and regional trade feed the same economy.

No isolated mini-game reward islands.

# 3. Fishing — a full profession, not a click timer

Fishing must be deep enough to become a primary playstyle.

## 3.1 Variables
Catch depends on:
- location;
- exact fishing spot;
- water type;
- time of day;
- weather;
- season/world state;
- bait;
- hook;
- rod;
- line;
- player fishing skill;
- fish behavior;
- time layer;
- rare event state.

## 3.2 Fishing interaction
Not rapid tapping.

Phases:
1. choose tackle;
2. cast;
3. read surface/current cues;
4. bite window;
5. hook;
6. tension management;
7. fish behavior pattern;
8. land or lose.

Fish can:
- make runs;
- change direction;
- dive;
- tire;
- fake a bite;
- cut weak line;
- react differently to rod action.

## 3.3 Fish data
Each species:
- habitat;
- size range;
- weight range;
- rarity;
- season;
- time;
- weather;
- preferred bait;
- fighting profile;
- sell value;
- cooking uses;
- quest uses;
- aquarium/display eligibility.

## 3.4 Rankings
Rankings should be fair and segmented.

Examples:
- largest species catch this week;
- heaviest fish;
- rarest catch;
- regional tournament;
- seasonal tournament;
- personal best;
- guild total.

Anti-cheat requires server authority once rankings are public.

## 3.5 Fishing economy
Caught fish can be:
- sold to NPC;
- sold to players;
- cooked;
- preserved;
- used as bait;
- displayed;
- donated to museum/aquarium;
- used in quests.

This creates real economic value.

# 4. Cooking

Cooking is both life content and combat preparation.

## 4.1 Inputs
- fish;
- vegetables;
- meat;
- spices;
- regional ingredients;
- rare time-layer ingredients.

## 4.2 Recipe discovery
Recipes come from:
- NPCs;
- experimentation;
- books;
- regional culture;
- quests;
- player trade;
- hidden discoveries.

## 4.3 Quality
Cooking quality can depend on:
- cooking skill;
- ingredient quality;
- freshness;
- cookware;
- recipe familiarity;
- optional timing/temperature mechanic.

## 4.4 Effects
Food should not be mandatory every minute.

Possible effects:
- max HEART;
- FOCUS recovery;
- resistances;
- fishing luck;
- gathering efficiency;
- travel endurance;
- crafting precision.

Rare foods may alter social/emote effects but never sell direct combat power for real money.

# 9. Jobs / roles

Jobs are deep identities, not cosmetic class labels.

Initial combat jobs should be few and mechanically strong.

Proposed original working set:

## WARDEN
Frontline protector.
- threat control;
- guard zones;
- intercept;
- shield timing;
- stance management.

## BREAKER
Physical attacker / disruption.
- weapon combos;
- stagger;
- part breaking;
- positional attacks.

## WEAVER
Support/control caster.
- buffs;
- debuffs;
- barriers;
- time-delay effects.

## LANTERN
Recovery / field support.
- healing;
- cleansing;
- recovery-over-time;
- light / detection utility.

## TRACE
Ranged tactical role.
- marking;
- traps;
- ranged attacks;
- enemy analysis.

## ECHO
High-risk magic attacker.
- elemental/resonance magic;
- cast timing;
- resource conversion;
- chain interactions.

Names are working terms and may change.

## Job depth
Each job needs:
- weapon families;
- active skills;
- passive traits;
- resource system;
- role-specific skill ceiling;
- solo tools;
- party identity.

No job should reduce to one optimal button sequence.

# 10. Magic system

Magic is not simply Fire I / Fire II copies.

Working concept:
**Resonance / 共鳴**

Magic interacts with:
- element;
- environment;
- status;
- time layer;
- previous party actions.

Elements may include:
- Heat;
- Flow;
- Gale;
- Stone;
- Light;
- Shade;
- Pulse;
- Stillness.

Examples:
- Flow effect makes a target more vulnerable to electrical/Pulse-style effects later;
- Heat can remove cold/stiff conditions but worsen certain volatile enemies;
- Stillness can delay an action but may strengthen an enemy that feeds on silence;
- Light reveals some ZURE entities rather than merely dealing damage.

Party combinations matter.

---

# 11. Skills and weapon mastery

Separate:
- character level;
- job level;
- weapon proficiency;
- profession level;
- knowledge/discovery.

A max-level character can still have unexplored weapon skills and professions.

Weapon families might include:
- blade;
- heavy weapon;
- polearm;
- shield;
- bow/ranged;
- focus/tool;
- unusual specialist weapons later.

Using a weapon develops proficiency and unlocks:
- techniques;
- timing windows;
- passive familiarity;
- situational bonuses.

Avoid mindless macro grinding as the optimal route.

---

# 12. Combat pacing

Target:
deliberate real-time or hybrid active-time combat.

Not twitch-action like a pure action game.
Not passive auto-attack waiting.

Initial design:
- basic attack rhythm;
- cooldown/charge abilities;
- cast times;
- global and off-global actions only if clarity remains;
- enemy telegraphs;
- interrupts;
- threat;
- positional play;
- limited stamina/Focus;
- item use with consequence.

Serious enemies require:
- observation;
- planning;
- role execution.

Trash mobs should still be dangerous in groups.

---

# 13. Enemy ecology

Enemies must be memorable inhabitants of the world.

Categories:
- wildlife;
- altered wildlife;
- ZURE anomalies;
- mechanical/urban oddities;
- time-layer entities;
- human/organized opposition when story requires.

Every meaningful enemy species gets:
- habitat;
- behavior;
- aggro rules;
- senses;
- diet/ecology note;
- attacks;
- weaknesses;
- resistances;
- drops;
- rare drop;
- time/weather changes;
- relationship to other creatures.

## Aggro examples
An enemy may detect:
- sight;
- sound;
- low HP;
- magic use;
- carrying a certain item;
- time-layer state.

This makes travel itself tactical.

---

# 14. Named enemies / notorious enemies

The world needs rare recognizable enemies.

Working model:
**Marked / 記名個体**

A Marked enemy:
- has a name;
- unique appearance variant;
- unusual behavior;
- valuable but not guaranteed reward;
- lore;
- spawn or trigger conditions.

Conditions may depend on:
- weather;
- time;
- prior kills;
- item;
- quest;
- location;
- ZURE state.

Players should share rumors about them.

Avoid simple fixed 5-minute respawn farming.

---

# 15. Boss design

Bosses combine:
- preparation;
- mechanics;
- enemy behavior;
- party roles;
- resource attrition.

Examples of mechanic types:
- interrupt priority;
- facing;
- safe zones;
- split groups;
- add control;
- status cleanse;
- environmental interaction;
- part break;
- time-layer shift.

A boss may have multiple viable solutions.

No single rigid choreography should dominate every encounter.

---

# 16. NPC depth

NPCs are not quest vending machines.

Each important NPC needs:
- occupation;
- schedule;
- relationships;
- opinion;
- likes/dislikes;
- location changes;
- story state;
- non-quest conversation;
- economic role where relevant.

NPC examples:
- bartender;
- fisher;
- carpenter;
- doctor;
- postal worker;
- cook;
- collector;
- mechanic;
- researcher;
- child;
- trader;
- traveler;
- retired adventurer.

They may:
- remember previous interactions;
- change prices;
- move house;
- disappear during story states;
- know different rumors by time/weather.

# 17. Social gameplay without progression pressure

Players should be able to spend an evening doing only:
- BAR conversations;
- fishing;
- cooking;
- market shopping;
- home decoration;
- sightseeing;
- screenshots;
- playing instruments/emotes later;
- helping new players;
- organizing a fishing tournament;
- attending a player gathering.

No main-story progress required.

---

# 18. Horizontal progression

Level alone cannot dominate.

Long-term power comes from:
- gear sets;
- resist sets;
- situational tools;
- weapon mastery;
- job combinations;
- consumables;
- knowledge;
- crafted specializations.

A veteran may carry multiple equipment sets for different content.

This creates preparation depth without infinite vertical level inflation.

---

# 19. Difficulty tiers

Open world:
- normal wildlife;
- dangerous zones;
- elite/Marked threats;
- areas where retreat is expected.

Group content:
- exploratory dungeon;
- advanced dungeon;
- high-difficulty hunt;
- raid-scale event later.

No automatic scaling that makes every enemy feel identical everywhere.

Dangerous areas should feel dangerous.

---

# 20. Information and discovery

Do not put every answer in the UI immediately.

Player knowledge systems:
- bestiary;
- fishing log;
- recipe book;
- map notes;
- ZURE journal;
- NPC rumor log;
- market history;
- personal notes later.

Discovering information is progression.

But hidden mechanics must still be learnable through fair clues.

---

# 21. Second-Life-style world layer

DOLZORE should become usable as a social place even by players who ignore combat.

Long-term systems:
- avatar customization;
- housing;
- neighborhoods;
- player BAR gatherings;
- player events;
- emotes;
- friend lists;
- groups;
- profiles;
- photo/screenshot mode;
- bulletin boards;
- optional user-created room decoration layouts.

Do NOT immediately add unrestricted user-generated scripting/content; moderation and security complexity are too high.

---

# 22. Relationship between BAR music commerce and game economy

Real-money music purchases remain external commerce.

They do NOT:
- grant combat stats;
- generate in-game currency;
- unlock progression;
- give rare fishing equipment.

BAR jukebox is the discovery/purchase interface.

In-game currency and real-money music sales remain structurally separate.

This avoids pay-to-win.

---

# 23. Backend implications

Single-player life systems:
can remain local.

Public rankings, player markets, houses visible to others, chat and MMO combat:
require authoritative backend.

Cloud architecture later:
- Durable Objects: zone/shard realtime state;
- D1: profiles, saves, market, rankings, housing metadata;
- object storage only when needed for larger assets/user-generated snapshots.

Fishing rankings MUST be server-authoritative.
Player market MUST be server-authoritative.
Tradable inventory MUST be server-authoritative.

Never trust browser-only state for competitive/economic systems.

# 25. Definition of success

The contradiction is intentional:

**The game should look easy to enter.**
**The game should take years to fully master.**

A new player sees:
a friendly pixel town.

A veteran sees:
- multiple gear sets;
- jobs;
- resistances;
- market history;
- rare fish conditions;
- housing projects;
- enemy ecology;
- time-layer routes;
- crafting specialization;
- group strategy;
- long-term social relationships.

That depth gap is a defining DOLZORE feature.
